# Thought Verifier

**Thought Verifier** is a simple framework for examining an idea or claim step by step.

Instead of immediately deciding whether an idea is right or wrong, the framework breaks it into several parts so that its reasoning can be inspected and improved.

## Purpose

The goal of this project is to make vague thinking more explicit.

A claim is analyzed through the following structure:

1. **Premise** — What assumptions does the idea depend on?
2. **Reasoning** — How do the premises lead to the conclusion?
3. **Conclusion** — What is the final claim?
4. **Counterexample** — What situation could challenge the claim?
5. **Weakness** — Where could the reasoning fail?
6. **Improvement** — How could the idea or reasoning be made stronger?
7. **One-line Summary** — Can the entire idea be expressed clearly in one sentence?

## Example

### Claim

> Putting more advertising banners in many locations will allow more people to see an advertisement.

### Premise

More banners placed in visible locations increase the number of opportunities for people to notice the advertisement.

### Reasoning

More locations
→ more potential viewers
→ greater exposure
→ potentially greater awareness.

### Conclusion

Increasing the number of well-placed banners may increase advertising exposure.

### Counterexample

If the banners are difficult to read, placed in low-traffic locations, or fail to attract attention, simply increasing their number may not improve advertising effectiveness.

### Weakness

The original idea assumes that exposure automatically leads to attention and participation.

### Improvement

Consider additional factors such as:

* readability
* location
* audience size
* repeated exposure
* online sharing
* word of mouth

### One-line Summary

**More advertising locations can increase exposure, but effectiveness depends on visibility, audience, and communication quality.**

## Why I Made This

I am interested in:

* structured reasoning
* algorithms
* problem solving
* AI systems

This project is an experiment in treating reasoning itself as something that can be structured, tested, and improved.

## Current Stage

This project is currently a **Markdown-based prototype**.

The current goal is to test the framework on many different kinds of ideas and identify which parts of the structure are useful and which need improvement.

## Future Ideas

Possible future directions include:

* creating more example analyses
* defining clearer evaluation rules
* comparing different reasoning patterns
* detecting missing assumptions
* turning the template into a small program
* experimenting with AI-assisted reasoning analysis

## Project Goal

The long-term goal is to explore a simple question:

**Can a repeatable structure help people examine and improve their own reasoning?**
